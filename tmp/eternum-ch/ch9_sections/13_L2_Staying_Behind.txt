TITLE: Staying Behind
LEVEL: 2
CHARS: 2896

Orion slept deeply until distant thunder pulled him back to consciousness, the promise of a coming storm echoing through the keep. His body ached pleasantly from Arannis’s brutal training, hunger already gnawing at him as he dragged himself from bed. A knock came at the door, and Arannis informed him that the Princess wished to see him regarding a portal home. The realization hit at once. He was finally leaving. Scrambling to dress, he barely had time to complain about elven clothing before Calypso quietly appeared inside the room, catching him off guard.

Calypso explained that she had gathered enough strength to open a portal for him, yet something about her felt unsettled. Orion noticed immediately and pressed her gently, offering a space where she could speak freely without courtly masks or politics. She admitted that returning to Hyril’ar felt wrong in a way she could not explain. Everything was unchanged, frozen in time, while she herself felt diminished. Her magic no longer answered her as it once had, and the weight of expectation pressed down harder than ever. At last, she confessed the truth she rarely allowed herself to acknowledge. She did not want to be a princess. She wanted freedom, movement, risk, and a life shaped by choice rather than duty, much like the time she had spent traveling with him.
[[File:Calypso rests in Orion's arms.png|thumb|Calypso rests in Orion's arms]]
Orion asked permission before pulling her into a hug, trying to ground her the only way he knew how. After a brief hesitation, she allowed it, leaning into him and returning the embrace before slowly pushing him away, steadier than before. The comfort did not change her obligations, but it cleared her thoughts enough to speak of what awaited her. She revealed that she was expected to choose a husband that very day, a political union meant to stabilize the realm and, according to the druids, help restore balance to her magic. The news stunned Orion, and he bristled at how quickly everything moved, yet Calypso accepted it with weary resolve. 

The moment was broken by the distant toll of a bell, signaling the arrival of the suitors. Calypso prepared to leave, thanking Orion for the brief peace he had given her. She opened a portal for him to Ekabar, promising that once her affairs were settled, she would come to him herself. As Orion stepped toward the portal, doubt crept in. Something about leaving now felt wrong. Turning back, he chose instead to stay and accompany her to the keep, refusing to let her face the ordeal alone. He framed it simply. You stay for people because they matter.

Calypso accepted, flustered but relieved, insisting he at least finish dressing properly. Together, they left the chamber side by side, Calypso granting him the role of her protector as they walked toward whatever awaited them next, both aware that this chapter was far from over.
