TITLE: The Elven Games
LEVEL: 2
CHARS: 4694

The Court Chamberlain announced the first trial of the noble arts: poetry. Each suitor, including the champion, was given one minute to compose a four line verse dedicated to Princess Calypso, with complete freedom over theme and structure. Orion struggled to even understand what was being asked as the first suitor, Fael of Thirinvale, stepped forward almost immediately and delivered a bold and provocative poem that drew a mix of admiration and surprise from the court. Calypso acknowledged it as daring yet poetic, with Regent Thorund openly praising its evocative nature.

Orym of Ilvenmyr followed, presenting a refined and elegant verse filled with imagery of nature and quiet reverence. His words were warmly received, earning sincere praise from both Calypso and the regents. Torion of Redveil came next, offering a harsh and violent poem centered on conquest and domination. While unsettling, parts of the court admired its raw intensity, and Calypso noted its visceral quality despite its brutality.

When Orion was finally called, the Chamberlain pressed him to either present or step aside. Orion moved forward anyway and delivered a simple, earnest poem focused on a small, sincere moment with Calypso. The court was divided, but Calypso openly stated that she liked it, surprising many and firmly halting any attempt to dismiss or destroy his work. With that, the Chamberlain declared the poetry trial concluded and announced the second noble art: painting.
[[File:Calypso and Lorelei talking.png|thumb|Calypso and Lorelei talking]]
Each participant was given an hour, a blank canvas, and fine paints. Orion struggled with doubt but ultimately chose to paint Calypso herself, using her presence as inspiration. As the painting continued, Calypso circulated the hall, clearly weary of the surrounding courtly chatter. During this time, she quietly confided in her maid Lorelei, expressing how deeply she disliked the endless ceremonies and how different things felt after spending time among humans. She admitted she missed the simplicity and honesty she had experienced with Orion, finding human interactions far more genuine than the calculated politeness of court life. Their conversation drifted into awkward curiosity and embarrassment before being cut short by the end of the painting trial.  

The Chamberlain and Calypso first examined Torion of Redveil’s work, a detailed depiction of his grandfather during an ancient war. Though skillful, it portrayed humans as monstrous figures, which Calypso quietly questioned before accepting the symbolic explanation. Orym of Ilvenmyr presented a highly accurate painting of Iskhaldrim, despite never having visited, earning strong praise for its precision. Fael of Thirinvale unveiled a confident self portrait depicting himself as a future king, a daring choice that impressed some and unsettled others.

When Orion’s painting was revealed, the Chamberlain openly mocked it as crude and childlike. Orion defended his effort, explaining he had never painted before. Calypso immediately intervened, declaring that she liked it and valued the intent behind it. She ordered that it not be destroyed and ended the debate, moving the court forward to the third noble art: music.

The Chamberlain announced that each suitor would perform an improvised piece on the lyre. Torion of Redveil performed first, stunning the court with an unexpectedly masterful and emotional melody. The performance was widely praised, including by Calypso herself. Orion followed last. Clearly outmatched, he gave an awkward and unconventional performance that shocked the court. While the Chamberlain reacted with outrage, Calypso defused the moment by calling it memorable and urging the court to move on.

The final trial was combat, conducted through a single archery shot using the same bow and arrow for all participants. Fael of Thirinvale struck near the inner ring, followed by an even better shot from Orym of Ilvenmyr. Torion of Redveil nearly hit the center, setting an intimidating standard.
[[File:Orion hits a bullseye .png|thumb|Orion hits a bullseye  ]]
Orion stepped forward last. Struggling with the unfamiliar weight of the bow, he steadied himself and focused, drawing on everything Arannis had taught him. His arrow struck the exact center of the target, even displacing Torion’s shot. The court was stunned. Calypso openly praised him, declaring that he had fully proven himself as the champion of Hyril’ar.  

With the trials concluded, the Chamberlain declared the day a celebration and ordered the feast to begin. As nobles gathered around Calypso and servants filled the hall, Orion quietly slipped away to the edge of the room.
