TITLE: The Interview
LEVEL: 2
CHARS: 2809

Powell led Orion down a hallway that felt more like a luxury hotel than an office building, stopping only when he opened a pair of golden double doors at the far end. Orion was immediately overwhelmed by the sheer excess of the place. It was Powell’s personal office, complete with a pool, and it struck Orion as the final boss of pure show-off wealth. Still, his focus snapped to the personal computer sitting inside. That was the real target. Nova’s USB needed to go into that machine, and all they had to do now was wait for Luna to act. The problem was that Powell clearly wanted the interviews wrapped up fast, so everything depended on timing.

Powell wasted no time asserting himself, skimming resumes and making it clear he cared less about grades and credentials than about the kind of people he let into his inner circle. He framed the interview as a test of ambition and efficiency, praising Orion’s age, degrees, and quick thinking while probing his motivations for wanting to work at Ulysses. Orion leaned fully into his cover, speaking about prestige, hard work, and long-term ambition, and even openly condemned William Bardot when pressed, which only seemed to amuse Powell. A quick mental math question followed, which Powell promptly dismissed as unimportant anyway, deciding he had Orion figured out.
[[File:Chang telling his story.png|thumb|Chang telling his story]]
Attention then shifted to Roberto Canchez, whose answer to the same question took a very different turn. Roberto shared a dramatic life story centered on war, loss, and survival, ultimately revealing that he himself was the child saved by a man who sacrificed everything to give him a future. The story clearly struck Powell on a deep emotional level, leaving him shaken and sentimental. Just as Powell tried to recover and move on, the power suddenly cut out. 
[[File:Nova's virus starting up.png|thumb|Nova's virus starting up]]
Inside, Orion felt a surge of relief. Luna had done her part. Powell excused himself to investigate the outage, stepping out of the office and sealing the door behind him. The moment he was gone, Chang pushed Orion into action. With only seconds before the backup generators kicked in, Orion powered up the device and plugged in Nova’s USB, trusting that the virus would handle the rest on its own. The countdown felt endless as the timer stalled, tension climbing with every second, until they were forced to bail at the very last moment. 

When Powell returned, the office was empty. He was informed that the two applicants had left, politely but pointedly criticizing the blackout as unprofessional. Frustrated and irritated, Powell dismissed the situation, ordered the next candidates in, and moved on, unaware that the real interview had never been the one he thought he was conducting.
