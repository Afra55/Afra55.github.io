TITLE: After the Suitors
LEVEL: 2
CHARS: 3373

A few hours later, Calypso finally sank into her chair, drained now that the halls were empty. She admitted that she had never imagined conversation alone could be so exhausting. Lorelei agreed, remarking that it was always worse when the heart was not truly in the exchange. Calypso thanked her for staying close through every tedious moment, and Lorelei replied that she would have remained even without duty compelling her. Calypso teased her about royal privileges and the extra glasses of Rivanor wine, which Lorelei quickly denied, insisting she could just as easily have stayed as Arannis’s daughter. Amused, Calypso withdrew the jab and dismissed her for the day, explaining that what remained now rested solely on her. She had to choose one of the three suitors to become her husband and the future king of Hyril’ar.

When asked how the meetings had gone, Calypso described them as hollow and ceremonial. Lord Fael hid crude innuendo behind grand promises of pleasure, Lord Orym spoke stiffly of duty and ancient family ties, and Lord Torion sounded more like a commander addressing a war council, obsessed with strength, blood, and warriors. Lorelei observed that none of them seemed to have truly moved her and wondered why Calypso could not refuse them as she had before. Calypso explained that the situation was different now. Her advisors feared the kingdom stood on the brink of war, unrest worsened by her long absence, and rumors claimed some old Houses might soon turn against the crown. A marriage might be the only way to preserve unity, and perhaps it was a debt she owed after so many years away. 

Lorelei argued that Hyril’ar was still strong, and that Calypso herself could defeat armies if needed, but Calypso hesitated and turned to her magic. She revealed that the Grand Druid believed Anima’s power within her was becoming volatile, yearning to be passed on and weakening her. Physical intimacy, the Druid claimed, could anchor it once more. Though uncomfortable, Calypso admitted this was another reason a suitor had to be chosen. Pressed to speak freely, Lorelei cautiously suggested that the act itself might matter more than the ceremony, but Calypso rejected the idea outright, warning that elven courts thrived on whispers and that any such secret would be weaponized against her. She added that she had already tried to resolve the matter alone, only to find her mind blocked and herself unable to find release.

Lorelei then hesitantly raised another possibility, mentioning Orion. Calypso reacted sharply, reminding her that he was human and that such a thing would be a grave crime under both law and ancient commandment. Lorelei argued that Orion was a stranger to the court and that Calypso had grown fond of him, but Calypso countered with the fate of Lady Arwil, stripped of her name and exiled after being discovered with a human. When Lorelei suggested Arwil’s downfall had come from carelessness, Calypso cut her off, declaring she would not allow a human to touch her, that she could not. Lorelei apologized and clarified that she had not meant physical contact at all, only that Orion’s presence might ease Calypso’s troubled thoughts. Calypso demanded an explanation, and Lorelei could only insist that she would do anything for her. Calypso answered with a quiet, unreadable sound, and the room settled into uneasy silence.
