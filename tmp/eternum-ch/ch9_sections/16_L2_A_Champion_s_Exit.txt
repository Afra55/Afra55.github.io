TITLE: A Champion's Exit
LEVEL: 2
CHARS: 2269

Orion quietly accepted that his role in the trials was finished. He felt he had done what he needed to do, and at the very least, Calypso had seemed genuinely relieved to have him at her side throughout the ordeal. With the feast underway, he decided it was best to return to his chambers before any of the suitors noticed something was off and realized he was not actually an elf.

Before he could leave, Torion of Redveil approached him and addressed him as Sir Grothelborn. Orion turned to face him and explained, somewhat awkwardly, that he could not remain at the feast due to a vow never to remove his helm in public, one he claimed was sworn to his late wife. Torion accepted this without question and took a moment to commend Orion’s exceptional archery, noting how impressive the shot had been even while wearing such heavy armor. He admitted that his own chances with Calypso were slim compared to Fael of Thirinvale or Orym of Ilvenmyr, but still extended an open invitation to Redveil should Orion’s path ever lead him there. Orion thanked him, surprised by the sincerity, and listened as Torion added that if rumors of civil war proved true, Redveil would stand with the crown and honor its old alliances. After they parted ways, Orion was left unsettled by the mention of civil war.

As Orion turned to leave, the Court Chamberlain stopped him and explained that he carried a message from Princess Calypso. She would spend the next few hours entertaining the guests and speaking further with the suitors, but she intended to meet Orion later in his chambers to open a passage back to his homeland before the day ended. Orion agreed easily and admitted he was looking forward to going home. The Chamberlain dryly remarked that he, too, looked forward to Orion’s departure, then grudgingly acknowledged that while Orion’s painting had been dreadful and his music painful to endure, his poem had been passable and his archery unexpectedly impressive, if perhaps aided by luck. Orion accepted the backhanded praise with good humor, thanked him, and finally took his leave.

On his way out, Orion decided he would either take a short nap or put in some extra longbow practice. After everything that had happened, he figured there was never a bad time to train.
